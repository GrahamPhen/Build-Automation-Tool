# How Shorts / TikTok / Reels recommend videos, and what improves retention and engagement

Researched 2026-10-15. Source quality warning: official platform statements are rare and mostly qualitative. Most numbers come from vendors (Buffer, Metricool, Socialinsider, Quso, Shortimize) and creator-tool blogs. Each item below is labelled as official, a data study, or a blog/anecdote.

## 1. What the platforms say about how short-form recommendations work (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
All three platforms rank short videos mainly on how viewers behave. The core signals are: whether people stop scrolling and keep watching (YouTube's "viewed vs swiped away" and engaged views), how much of the video they watch or finish (TikTok calls finishing a strong signal; Instagram's top signal is watch time), and active responses (shares or DM sends, likes, comments, follows). Instagram explicitly favours original content and limits reach for reposted or watermarked clips. On YouTube, the March 2025 change to how views are counted changed the reported numbers, not how videos are distributed.

### Cited Findings
**YouTube (official or near-official)**
- From March 31, 2025, a Shorts "view" counts as soon as the video starts or replays, with no minimum watch time. The old metric was kept and renamed "engaged views", described as "how many viewers choose to continue watching your Shorts". Engaged views still drive YPP eligibility and earnings. — [TeamYouTube Help Community post](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/333869549/a-change-to-how-we-count-views-on-shorts?hl=en), as quoted by [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/); also [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/03/27/how-does-youtube-shorts-count-views-2025/)
- Engaged views exclude loops. They are in YouTube Analytics Advanced Mode and are the right metric for comparing videos from before and after March 31. — [Shortimize (blog)](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate); [Sprout Social help](https://support.sproutsocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/35874991211533-YouTube-Shorts-View-Count-Update-March-2025)
- YouTube Studio's Shorts "Viewed vs. swiped away" report shows the share of feed impressions where the viewer kept watching instead of swiping on immediately. YouTube does not publish how many seconds count as "viewed". — [Creator Essentials glossary](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/viewed-vs-swiped-away/); [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate)
- Todd Sherman (Director of Shorts Product Management) on Creator Insider: Shorts recommendations are more varied than long-form because users watch hundreds of Shorts, and a swipe does not always count as a view. His summary was "The audience is the algorithm". (2023 interview, older material.) — [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/youtube-shorts-algorithmus-creator-insights); [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/youtube-explains-how-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-works/)
- Shorts can run up to 3 minutes (announced Oct 3, 2024; effective Oct 15, 2024), for square or vertical video. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-expands-3-minute-shorts-to-all-users/736967/); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/youtube-shorts-expands-to-three-minute-videos-enhancing-creator-flexibility-44082.htm)
- Google Ads help (about Shorts *ads*, not organic posts): 9:16 vertical "deliver[s] better performance" than landscape. Only the first 180 s play in the Shorts feed, and under 60 s is recommended "to align with user behavior on the Shorts feed". — [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697?hl=en)

**TikTok (official, but dated June 2020)**
- For You ranking uses user interactions (likes, shares, follows, comments, videos the user creates), video information (captions, sounds, hashtags) and device/account settings. Device/account settings carry lower weight. — [TikTok Newsroom, "How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou"](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/)
- Strong signals outweigh weak ones. TikTok's example of a strong signal is "whether a user finishes watching a longer video from beginning to end". — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/)
- "Neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors." The feed avoids showing two videos in a row with the same sound or from the same creator, and leaves out duplicated content and videos the user has already seen. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/)

**Instagram (Mosseri, January 2025 video series, via press coverage)**
- The top three ranking signals are watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach (DM shares). "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." Connected means followers. Unconnected means people reached through recommendations. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/); [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-reveals-key-factors-for-boosting-reach-8646916)
- Sources disagree on which signal ranks first. Most put watch time first. One July 2026 blog puts sends first. A 2024 Mosseri remark said sends per reach "correlate more, in my experience, with overall reach than anything else". That was a personal view, not a formal ranking. — [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389); [posteverywhere.ai (blog)](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-the-instagram-algorithm-works)
- Instagram gives more credit to original content than to reposts, and has tested notices telling creators when a watermark (in practice, a TikTok logo) is limiting a Reel's reach. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2551088); [t3n](https://t3n.de/news/?p=1671110)
- Unverified blog claims: original content gets "40-60% more distribution", and accounts with 10+ reposts in 30 days are excluded from recommendations. No primary source was found. — [dataslayer.ai](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers)

### Inferences
- All three platforms use the same funnel. First, does the viewer stop scrolling (the first frame and first second)? Then, how much of the video do they watch (retention or completion)? Then, do they act on it (share, like, follow)? For a Minecraft timelapse, a strong first frame matters more than anything at the end.
- Upload clean files with no watermark to each platform (no TikTok logo on Reels). Instagram penalises recycled, watermarked content.
- On YouTube, judge videos by engaged views and the viewed-vs-swiped rate, not raw views. Raw views now include instant swipes and loops.

### Gaps
- No official 2025-2026 TikTok document lists its current ranking signals. The newsroom explainer is from 2020.
- YouTube has not published how it weights swipe-away against average % viewed. No official YouTube Help page on Shorts ranking was retrieved; only the Help Community post was.
- Mosseri's original video wording was not retrieved directly; only press quotes were.

## 2. Retention benchmarks and best-performing length

### Takeaway
No independent, rigorous benchmark exists. Vendor data puts the median Short at about 40-45% average viewed, with top performers above 70-90%. Length studies conflict. Buffer (TikTok) and a small Inflow study found longer videos (over 50-60 s) got more reach and views. Other analyses say shorter clips hold a higher share of viewers. The defensible rule is to make a video only as long as its content holds attention, and to compare it with your own videos of similar length.

### Cited Findings
- **Quso** (about 508K posts from 1,900+ creators): median Short about 45% average view-through. The top 10% average about 91%, the rest about 40%. "40-45% is average, 50-60%+ is doing well." — [Quso research](https://quso.ai/research/video-retention-benchmarks)
- **vidIQ's own channel**: 73.6% average percentage viewed on its Shorts over the 12 months to June 2026. This is one channel, not a benchmark. — cited via [GoFaceless](https://www.gofaceless.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate-benchmarks); see also [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/)
- **Shortimize**: Shorts with 1M+ views average about 76% retention. — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/how-to-analyze-youtube-shorts-performance)
- **GoFaceless / ClipIQ framework** (thresholds set by the framework, not measured): 50-64% average, 65-79% strong, 80%+ viral. Under 15 s, aim for more than 65%. For 30-60 s, a completion rate above 30% is strong. — [GoFaceless](https://www.gofaceless.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate-benchmarks)
- Bytecap says its own benchmark page is an "evidence synthesis—not a ... customer-performance study". — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)
- **Buffer** (1.1M TikToks): videos over 60 s got 43.2% more reach and 63.8% more watch time than shorter ones, 70.3% more reach than 10-30 s videos, and 95.7% more reach than 5-10 s clips. Buffer's data scientist warns length "isn't necessarily the determining factor". The study's date was not confirmed. — reported via [Metricool/Buffer coverage](https://metricool.com/instagram-reels-length) and [joinstatus](https://www.joinstatus.com/blog-for-brands/tiktok-reels-length)
- **Inflow Network** (5,400 Shorts, secondhand, unverified): 50-60 s Shorts got about 22x the views of clips under 10 s. — [ytshark](https://ytshark.com/?p=499)
- **Socialinsider, 2023** (old): TikTok engagement drops after 15 s. — via [joinstatus](https://www.joinstatus.com/blog-for-brands/tiktok-reels-length)
- **Metricool 2025** (5.6M videos): average TikTok watch time is only 3.75 s. On Instagram, Reels views fell 59% year on year. On YouTube, Shorts engagement fell 36% even though 61% more Shorts were published. — [Metricool report](https://metricool.com/social-media-short-video-report-2025/); [Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/press-release/20250908-metricool-2025-study-unveils-70-surge-in-short-form-video-as-tiktok-conti)
- **Socialinsider** (69M videos, Jan 2025-Jul 2026, published Aug 2026): average engagement rate in 2026 was TikTok 2.60%, Reels 0.45%, Shorts 0.30%. Shorts get far more views at small follower counts: under 5K followers, about 15,160 average views on Shorts vs 625 on Reels and 350 on TikTok. — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts/)

### Inferences
- A 30-60 s Minecraft build timelapse aiming for 60%+ average viewed would be above the vendor median. 70%+ is in high-performer territory.
- Percentages from short and long videos are not comparable, because short clips loop and so post inflated percentages. Compare our takes against each other at similar lengths.
- Socialinsider's figures suggest YouTube Shorts gives new or small channels the most reach of the three platforms.

### Gaps
- No vendor dataset gives retention by niche (Minecraft or gaming).
- No study found on the best length for YouTube Shorts specifically after the 3-minute limit.

## 3. Hooks, on-screen text, looping, music vs original audio, trending sounds, end cards and CTAs

### Takeaway
Official guidance (TikTok's ads guidance) says to put the core message in the first 3 seconds and the hook within 6. It also recommends text overlays (5-10 words per second) and leaning into trending sounds. Creator consensus is stricter: the first 1-2 seconds and the first frame decide whether viewers swipe. Looping and "end in a way that replays" advice is widespread but only anecdotal. No controlled study shows that end cards or CTAs hurt retention. The practitioner view is that long or interrupting CTAs cause visible drop-offs, while short ones are fine.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok (official, ads guidance): "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds" and "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds". Use captions or text overlays at "5-10 words per second", plus transitions, stickers and graphics to hold attention. — [TikTok Ads Help, Creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- TikTok (official, ads): "Lean into trends", using Creative Center trending hashtags and the Commercial Music Library. 77% of users say they like brands using trends (TikTok's own survey). 90% of ad recall impact happens in the first 6 seconds. These are ad metrics, not organic ones. — [TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices); [TikTok for Business blog](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)
- TikTok's official ranking signals include "sounds" as video information, and the feed avoids showing two same-sound videos in a row. — [TikTok Newsroom (2020)](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/)
- YouTube Shorts templates and the October 2024 update emphasise pairing clips with popular sounds. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/youtube-shorts-expands-to-three-minute-videos-enhancing-creator-flexibility-44082.htm)
- Common causes of high swipe-away rates: the first frame doesn't show the subject, an opening greeting or logo, and small or crowded on-screen text. Some swipes are just lack of interest in the topic. (Blog.) — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate); [Creator Essentials](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/swiped-away/)
- Blogs advise hooking within 1-2 s, changing visuals about every 4 s, and designing seamless loops. One vendor claims a 30 s Short watched twice beats a 60 s Short watched once. All of this is anecdotal. — [JoinBrands](https://joinbrands.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices/); [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/scripting-youtube-shorts-engagement); [Teleprompter](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-to-go-viral-on-youtube-shorts)
- CTAs: no controlled study was found. Practitioners say aggressive or long CTAs reduce trust and step on the payoff, and advise checking the retention graph for a drop where the CTA appears. — [ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide); [GoFaceless](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/high-retention-youtube-shorts-attributes)
- A pinned comment with a link reportedly converts 1-3% (claim with no stated method). — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-shorts-hurting-long-form-views)

### Inferences
- For a build timelapse, open on motion plus a clear preview of the finished build, or a striking first frame. Do not open on an empty pad or a title card. A seamless ending that cuts back to the start can raise loops. Loops raise total views, but YouTube's engaged views exclude loops.
- An outro of several seconds (a server address card, a logo) sits at the point where viewers decide to swipe or replay. It probably lowers completion and the share of viewers who loop. Keep any promo to about 1-2 s, overlay it on the final reveal instead of adding a separate card, or move it to the pinned comment or description.
- Trending or licensed music may help TikTok and Reels discovery, since sound is an official ranking feature there. On YouTube, using library or "popular sounds" through the Shorts tools is the safe route.

### Gaps
- No controlled data on music vs original audio for organic reach.
- No quantified study of the effect of looping, or of seamless endings, on distribution.
- No organic (non-ad) official TikTok Creator Academy page on hooks or captions was retrieved.

## 4. Titles, descriptions, hashtags and custom covers or thumbnails

### Takeaway
In the swipe feed, YouTube Shorts thumbnails are not shown, and the title appears only as small overlay text. Titles and thumbnails matter for views outside the feed (search, the channel page, hashtag and audio pages, the Subscriptions feed). TikTok officially uses captions and hashtags as video information for categorisation. Hashtags work as topic labels more than reach boosters.

### Cited Findings
- Most Shorts views come from the feed, where thumbnails are not shown. Thumbnails do appear in search, on hashtag pages, on audio pivot pages and on the channel page. Thumbnail control was limited: frame selection on mobile, with fuller custom thumbnails rolling out gradually. — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-titles-thumbnails-matter); [PhoneArena](https://phonearena.com/news/youtube-launches-features-for-creators-to-control-the-look-of-their-shorts-thumbnails_id162189)
- A YouTube Creator Liaison post confirms Shorts appear in Search, on the Home and Channel pages, and in the Subs feed. In those places, titles and thumbnails work much as they do for long-form. — [YouTube blog, Rene's top five (Jul 2024)](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/renes-top-five-july-25-2024/)
- TikTok lists "captions, sounds, and hashtags" as video-information ranking signals. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/)

### Inferences
- Shorts titles should be short, searchable descriptions ("Minecraft cozy cottage build timelapse"). A 1-3 word on-video text label works better than a long title, because the title is barely visible in the feed. A custom cover still matters for the channel grid and for Instagram's profile grid.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study on the effect of Shorts titles or hashtags. Todd Sherman's hashtag comments weren't retrieved.
- Instagram cover-image impact: no data found.

## 5. Promotional CTAs (e.g. a Minecraft server address at the end)

### Takeaway
No platform statement or data study measures how much promotional CTAs cost in organic Shorts. The indirect evidence points one way: ranking rewards completion, replays and shares, and practitioners see drops where CTAs interrupt. That favours brief, integrated or off-video promotion over a separate end card.

### Cited Findings
- No controlled study found. Practitioners say long or aggressive CTAs reduce trust and step on the payoff. — [ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide)
- A vendor says a simple end CTA can lift interaction, but gives no retention data. — [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/resource/youtube-shorts-analytics)
- Links in a pinned comment are said to convert better than in-video features (1-3%, no stated method). — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-shorts-hurting-long-form-views)
- TikTok says finishing a video is a strong signal, and Instagram ranks watch time as a top signal. Both are cited in section 1. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)

### Inferences
- Put the server address as small persistent corner text or a 1-2 s overlay on the reveal shot, and also in the description and pinned comment. Avoid a black or static outro card. A/B test across takes: compare viewed-vs-swiped and average % viewed with and without the outro.

### Gaps
- No platform rules found on promoting game servers. Platform policies on commercial content were not researched.

## 6. Posting frequency and series consistency

### Takeaway
Buffer's large correlational studies show diminishing returns. On TikTok, 2-5 posts a week gives the steepest gain in views per post. On Instagram, 3-5 posts a week is the suggested sweet spot. Median views per post stay flat however often an account posts, so the gains come from more chances at an outlier hit. TikTok says follower count and past hits are not direct ranking factors, so each video competes largely on its own merits.

### Cited Findings
- Buffer, TikTok (11.4M posts, 150K+ accounts, fixed-effects regression, around October 2025): compared with posting once a week, 2-5 posts a week gave +17% views per post, 6-10 gave +29%, and 11+ gave +34%. Median views stay at about 500 regardless of frequency. — [Search Engine Journal](https://beta.searchenginejournal.com/study-shows-2-5-weekly-tiktoks-deliver-biggest-view-increase/558641/); [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/tiktok-sweet-spot-new-study-shows-2-5-weekly-posts-maximize-viewing-efficiency/)
- Buffer, Instagram (2M+ posts, 100K+ accounts, August 2025): "posting 3-5 times per week ... is likely the sweet spot" for reach and growth. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)
- TikTok: follower count and previous high-performing videos are not direct factors. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/)
- A report finds frequent posting is linked to faster YouTube channel growth. Details were not verified. — [Eulogy summary](https://www.eulogy.co.uk/trending-tuesdays/19-08-25)

### Inferences
- A steady 3-5 build Shorts per week per platform is supported by the evidence. Daily posting adds some upside if quality holds. Series consistency (the same format, recognisable opening) has no direct data, but it fits how ranking works: viewers who liked one build tend to be shown the next.

### Gaps
- No YouTube Shorts-specific frequency study was retrieved. No data was found on how series or playlists affect Shorts distribution.
